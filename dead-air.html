Walking the American WayCommentary 13.4 · Abhayarājakumāra

Dead Air

…come, children, let us shut up the box and the puppets, for our play is played out.
— Thackeray, Vanity Fair

As it stands in the log, at the top of the Abbott Hotel.

I. The Proclamation

Ruby Wells, Nevada · Sunday, September 12, 1943

STATION KRWN · 250 WATTS · 1240 KILOCYCLES · PROGRAM AND OPERATING LOG · OPERATOR ON DUTY: E. LAIRD

6:00 a.m.Sign on. Anthem, from transcription. Plate current normal. Frost on the tower guys already.

11:00First Methodist, by wire. Line good.

2:00 p.m.Remote from the courthouse steps. The Mayor proclaims the seven days from today through Saturday the eighteenth to be Lieutenant Wade Abbott Week in Ruby Wells. County quota in the Third War Loan is $410,000. Lt. Abbott, 7th Division, was hit at Attu in May and is out of the hospital on leave. He is presented with a key to the city, gilt, about a foot long. He says thank you and one other sentence, which the wind took.

2:11Mr. C. B. Abbott, the Lieutenant’s father, owner of this hotel and president of this company, announces that his son is to want for nothing in this town for the week. Also that he has had the floor show in the Sagebrush Room held over through Saturday at his own expense, to play the bond rallies, and that Miss June Darrow of that show, taps and songs, has agreed to appear at each one with the Lieutenant.

2:14Somebody puts a pasteboard crown on the Lieutenant, left over from the Elks’ carnival. He leaves it on. Crowd estimated at nine hundred, which is most of us.

2:20Return to studio. Remarks: he looks nineteen. He is twenty-four. He kept turning the key over to see what it was made of.

12:00Sign off. Anthem.

II. The Week

Monday, September 13, to Saturday, September 18

Mon. 8:00Rally, Sagebrush Room, by remote. Lt. Abbott speaks forty seconds. Miss Darrow, two choruses and a tap routine. I put the second microphone on the floor by her feet. The taps come over clean.

Mon. 8:40Miss Darrow comes up the four flights after the show to ask how it sounded. Told her, like a good fist on a key. She had me explain that. Nine years on the boats I knew every operator on the Lakes by his sending before he gave his call, and a hand is a hand. She says she has been on the road since she was fifteen, is twenty-seven, and sends twenty dollars a week to her mother in Scranton. She asks to see how a record is cut and watches a whole side.

Tues. 12:15Rotary, by remote. The same. The Lieutenant still has the crown, in his blouse pocket now, folded.

Wed. 12:00Southern Pacific roundhouse, noon whistle. She dances on a baggage truck. Bond sales to date $171,000.

Thurs. 7:30High school gymnasium. Lt. Abbott excused from speaking at his own request. He stands.

Fri. 9:15Remarks. He has not been out of this building since Monday except in his father’s car to a rally and back. He takes his meals in the Sagebrush Room and is at the front table for both shows, every night. People send drinks. The barber came up to him. His money is no good anywhere in the county, and they have started calling him the King, to his face, and he answers to it.

Sat. 2:00Parade, described from the hotel marquee. The Lieutenant rides in Mr. Abbott’s open Packard with the crown on, Miss Darrow beside him at the Treasury’s request. The Stockmen’s Association float is a gold paper saddle eleven feet high. Bond sales $388,000.

Sat. 12:00Sign off. By the proclamation the week ends now.

III. The Eighth Day

Riverside Park band shell · Sunday, September 19

5:05 p.m.Lt. Abbott at the control room door with his hair wet. He has been swimming in the Humboldt below the bridge, the first time he has been in water since the Aleutians, he says. Asks if we are carrying the band concert tonight. We are.

8:00Remote, Riverside Park. Municipal Band, closing bond appeal. The floor show leaves on Number 27 at 11:40 for Reno. Miss Darrow is not on the bill. She is doing one number because he asked her. I set the floor microphone on the band shell planks myself. He is on a bench under the cottonwoods, front row. Cutting the whole program on a sixteen-inch blank for the Treasury report.

8:41:10Miss Darrow, vocal, one chorus, with the band.

8:42:05Taps begin. Floor microphone up.

8:42:51Taps stop in the middle of a bar.

8:42:51to 8:43:14. Dead air on the floor microphone, twenty-three seconds. The band plays on eleven bars, not knowing, and quits a man at a time.

8:43:14Cut to studio. Mrs. Pease at the organ.

9:30Dr. Kessler by telephone. She put her hand to her side like a person cut, sat down on the planks, and was gone before he got up the steps. He thinks an artery, and that it had been waiting on her since she was a child. Nothing anybody there could have done. Announcement held, by order of Mr. Abbott, until her mother is reached.

11:40Number 27 on time. The show went without her.

12:00Sign off. Disc marked No. 2,114 and shelved.

IV. After Sign-Off

The control room · Monday, September 20, ten past one in the morning

Carrier is off. This sheet is not for the Commission, and I will take it out of the binder.

The Lieutenant came up the four flights at 1:10. His father has been looking for him since nine. So have the Methodist minister and an Army doctor they got on the telephone from Reno. He told the night clerk he would not see any of them, and that there was one man in town who could do him any good, because one man had heard it right. Then he came up here.

You have it on a record. Play it.

Played No. 2,114 from 8:41. He stood at the turntable. At fifty-one seconds she stops. Played it again at his asking. He asked a third time, and before I set the arm down I said what I had to say. I put it here as near as I can.

I was nine years with the phones on before there was a voice on the air. I have heard a hand quit sending in the middle of a word. You know the man by his fist, and then there is only the carrier, and then not that. I kept count of most things in those days, and I lost count of that. There are two thousand one hundred platters in that closet, and on every one of them somebody stops. She stops on this one at fifty-one seconds past the minute. She will stop there every time you have me play it. Son, she has stopped more times than there are records, and you have stood where you are standing and cried over it more times than that. There is no meter on this board that will read it.

He did not ask how I knew that. I could not have told him. He sat down on the transcription cabinet and was quieter than he had been.

I told him not to go under in it. A man can drown in a thing like this, or he can come and look at how it is put together. I took him across the hall into Studio A and put all the lights on.

It is a bare room, eighteen by twenty-four, with egg cartons glued to the walls. I showed him the works. The coach and four that the children hear on Saturday mornings is two halves of a coconut in a tray of gravel and a strap of sleigh bells. The thunder is a sheet of tin on a nail. The rain is birdseed on a drumhead. There is a door in a frame that opens on nothing. The ten thousand people cheering are on a platter, and so are the one man laughing and the dog. Lieutenant Abbott Week came out of this room and a hundred and eighty feet of pipe on the roof, and I showed him that from the window, with the red lamp on top. He had the key in his hand again. I told him it was plaster. He said he knew that Tuesday.

I keep one book up here for the long nights. I read him the front of it and the back of it, standing at the glass.

As the manager of the Performance sits before the curtain on the boards and looks into the Fair, a feeling of profound melancholy comes over him in his survey of the bustling place.

A man with a reflective turn of mind, walking through an exhibition of this sort, will not be oppressed, I take it, by his own or other people’s hilarity.

When you come home you sit down in a sober, contemplative, not uncharitable frame of mind, and apply yourself to your books or your business.

— W. M. Thackeray, Vanity Fair (1848), “Before the Curtain”

Ah! Vanitas Vanitatum! which of us is happy in this world? Which of us has his desire? or, having it, is satisfied?—come, children, let us shut up the box and the puppets, for our play is played out.

— Vanity Fair, the last lines

He looked a good while through the glass at the empty studio. Then he took the crown out of his blouse pocket, unfolded it, and set it on the sound table beside the coconuts.

He did not ask for the record again. I offered it to him. I told him it was glass under the lacquer, they are all glass since the aluminum went to the war, and that he would have to carry it flat. He put his hand out for it and then did not take it. He said it was hers and I should keep it where I kept the others.

2:35, he went down. I heard him knock at his father’s door on the third floor.

V. Shelf Three

Station KRWN · later sheets

Sept. 20, 6:00 a.m.Sign on. Anthem. Plate current normal.

7:15Announcement of the death of Miss June Darrow, of Scranton, Pennsylvania, read by Lt. Abbott at his own asking. He read it level. Mrs. Pease played after.

Sept. 26Lt. Abbott reported back for duty. County closed the drive at $431,000.

Nov. 4, 1945Wade Abbott on the board, first shift alone. He has the ear for it. I have him on Tuesdays and Thursdays and the Saturday children’s hour, where he does the coach.

Mar. 9, 1946Remarks. He asked today where No. 2,114 was. Told him the closet, third shelf, where it has been. He said all right. He did not go and look. He put the birdseed back in its can and pulled the switch at midnight himself.

Background

The verse

Come, look at this glittering world, like unto a royal chariot; the foolish are immersed in it, but the wise do not touch it.Dhammapada 171, tr. F. Max Müller (1881)

In the commentary, Prince Abhaya puts down a rising on the frontier, and his father the king rewards him with the kingdom for seven days and with a celebrated dancer and singer for his court. For seven days he does not leave the palace. On the eighth he bathes at the river, sits down in his pleasure garden to watch her perform, and she is seized with cutting pains and dies as she dances. He is sure no one but the Buddha can put out his grief. The Buddha tells him there is no counting how many times she has died this way in the round of births, and no measuring the tears he has shed over her, and that only the simple sink in sorrow; then he speaks the verse of the painted chariot. A two-hundred-fifty-watt station, 1943

United States troops of the 7th Infantry Division landed on Attu, in the Aleutians, on May 11, 1943, and fought there in fog and snow until the end of the month. The Treasury’s Third War Loan drive ran from September 9 to the beginning of October that year (see Series E bonds), with quotas down to the county, and towns built rallies, parades and “weeks” around any decorated man home on leave. Ruby Wells, its hotel and station KRWN are invented; the country is that of the Humboldt River and the Southern Pacific main line around Elko, Nevada.

Small stations of the time were required to keep a program log and an operating log for the Federal Communications Commission, minute by minute, and many recorded their own broadcasts on sixteen-inch lacquer discs, called electrical transcriptions. During the war, when aluminum was a critical material, the discs were made on a glass core. Tap dancers really did perform on the air, with a microphone set at the feet. The sound effects in Studio A are the ordinary ones of the trade. A “fist” is a telegrapher’s word for the personal rhythm of an operator’s sending. The engineer’s book is Thackeray’s Vanity Fair (1848), whose narrator calls himself the manager of a puppet show at a fair; the passages are from its preface and its last paragraph, as in the Project Gutenberg text.
