Walking the American WayCommentary 26.6 · Aññatara Pabbajita

Hatched at Home

That bird of self-love which was hatched at home, if it be not looked to, will eat out the life of all grace and goodness.
— Robert Cushman, sermon preached at Plymouth, 1621

Thus I heard it, at New Plymouth.

I. The Removals

New Plymouth · May 1638

By the spring of 1638 the town was going over the water. Every week a cart went up the shore toward Duxbury with a bedstead roped on it legs up and a kettle wedged between the legs, and every week a shallop went off from the landing toward Greens Harbor with somebody's chests to the gunwales. Nobody was leaving the colony. They were only leaving the town. Kine sold at twenty pound in the Bay that year, some at twenty-eight, and a man with kine had to have meadow, and the meadow was across the bay. The houses they left stood with the doors pegged, and grass came up in the dooryards, and the street that had lived close under the fort hill for seventeen years was, as the old planters said to one another, very thin.

The Widow Ware did not remove. She was sixty-eight. She had walked out of Nottinghamshire in 1608 with the others and lived fourteen years in Leiden, where she learned to sit with the sick, and had come over in the Anne in the summer of 1623 with her husband Thomas, a cooper, and two boys. Thomas died in the infectious fever of 1633, the year the great flies came up out of holes in the ground and made the woods ring. Both her sons had gone to Duxbury with their cattle and wanted her there. She kept her house on the street and her garden and her four hens, and went to anyone who sent.

Rowland Fenn came in the last week of May leading two she-kids on a cord.

He was a Norfolk man of about fifty, long in the arm, with a beard he cut himself with shears. He had come out of the bishop’s church at home when the new bishop at Norwich began putting the godly ministers out of their pulpits, and out of his brother’s house over the same quarrel, and had joined the church at Boston in 1636 and come out of that too, in the winter they took the guns from the men who held with Mrs. Hutchinson. He would not go with them to the island in the spring. He came south instead into Plymouth’s patent and hired a neck of rough ground beyond the Jones River that nobody wanted yet, and kept goats on it, and lived in a hut of his own building with no glass in the window. He did not come to meeting. He called himself a seeker, by which he meant that the true church had not been gathered anywhere on the earth since the apostles died, and he would know it when it was.

She-kids were going at thirty shillings that spring and often at forty, because every family that went over the water wanted a milch goat to carry them until their cows came in. Rowland got forty apiece at the landing from a Greens Harbor man, in silver, and counted it into his purse, and took it out and counted it again.

The shallop was loading. An old planter who had come in the first ship stood on the landing to see the family off, and when the wife began to cry he said what the old planters said at every removal. Never mind it. We are but strangers and pilgrims, wherever we lie down.

Rowland Fenn laughed out loud, and the old man turned and looked at him, and Rowland put his purse away and went up the street to the Widow Ware’s.

•••

He ate at her table whenever he came in. In February he had lain three days with an ague on the neck with nobody to milk, and somebody had told her, and she had walked out to him over the river ice and sat with him and milked his goats and given him broth with a spoon. Since then he came to her door. He came, too, because she was the one person on the street who would let him talk.

Did you hear the old man at the landing?

I wasn’t at the landing.

Strangers and pilgrims. They go over the bay for grass and call it a pilgrimage. He broke his bread into the pottage. Your people tell everybody they came out. Separate, they say. Out of what? They came out of England in a body, with their elder and their pots and their quarrels, and sat down here in a body. Now they go out of Plymouth for cows. He held up his hand and counted on it. I came out of the bishop’s church. I came out of my brother’s house. I came out of Boston. I would not go to the island. I’ll not come into yours. If any man in this patent has gone out, Goodwife, it’s I.

She cut him more bread.

You have five, she said.

Five what?

On your hand. Eat.

II. Platters and Dishes

The first of June

He came again on the first day of June with two more kids, because the Greens Harbor man’s brother wanted a pair, and after he had sold them he sat at her table in the afternoon with the door open for the warmth and went on with it. He had been going on with it since Candlemas. She sat by the door mending a shift and let him.

Come out from among them, and separate yourselves, saith the Lord. It’s the whole ground your people stand on. And I’m the only one in this patent that’s done it.

She bit the thread.

The noise came before anything else. It came out of the north, low, a murmur like thunder a long way off over the woods, and they both looked toward the door to see the cloud, and there was no cloud. Then it was nearer and under them. The pewter on the shelf above the hearth began to talk, platter against platter, and walked, and three of them went off the edge one after another and rang on the hearthstones. The pail by the door slopped. The whole house leaned and came back like a ship.

She was out of the door before he was off the bench. Outside the street was moving the way a blanket moves when somebody under it turns over. She took hold of the pales of her garden fence with both hands and held on and the pales held on to the ground. Rowland stood in the doorway gripping the post. Down the street the women were all out, each one holding to a post or a pale, and a chimney top at the far end let go its clay in a long slide, and a dog went past under the fences on its belly, and the hens flew up onto the woodpile, and then it eased, and then it stopped.

Nobody spoke for a while. Then everybody did.

Goodwife Atwood from next door came along the fence with her hand on her chest. The chief men were all met in one house, she said, with the friends that are removing. It took them in the middle of their talk. Did you ever. As if the Lord would show us what He thinks of our shaking ourselves to pieces.

Rowland let go of the post. His face was white above the beard, but he was smiling. There, Goodwife, he said to the widow. There’s the answer to your pilgrims. He shakes them for their cows.

The Widow Ware went in and picked her platters up off the hearth. One had a dent in the rim where it had struck the firedog. She pressed at the dent with her thumb.

He shook my platters, she said, and I am not removing. And He shook you, that has gone out of everything.

He began to answer and she held up her hand with the platter in it.

Mr. Cushman stood up here the first winter, she said, when the Fortune came, and preached to the ones that were left alive. I wasn’t here. Thomas bought the little book in London the year after, with no name on it, and read it to me all the way over in the Anne, and there’s nobody in this town didn’t know whose it was. I’ll say you the piece I have.

She did not say it the way a minister says a thing. She said it the way she said what was in a broth, looking at the dent.

It is indeed a matter of some commendation for a man to remove himself out of a thronged place into a wide wilderness; to take in hand so long and dangerous a journey, to be an instrument to carry the Gospel and humanity among the brutish heathen; but there may be many goodly shews and glosses and yet a pad in the straw, men may make a great appearance of respect unto God, and yet but dissemble with him, having their own lusts carrying them: … let the shew be what it will, the substance is naught, and that bird of self-love which was hatched at home, if it be not looked to, will eat out the life of all grace and goodness: and though men have escaped the danger of the sea, and that cruel mortality, which swept away so many of our loving friends and brethren; yet except they purge out this self-love, a worse mischief is prepared for them …

— Robert Cushman, The Sin and Danger of Self-Love, preached at Plymouth, 1621

We were never called anything for the walking, she said. A body that has walked out of Scrooby, and out of Amsterdam, and out of Leiden, and over the sea, is a body that has walked. Nobody is gone out for that. He is gone out when the thing is gone out of him. She set the platter back on the shelf. Come out from among them, and separate yourselves. You’ve said it to me at this table since Candlemas. You never say the rest of the verse.

He knew the rest of the verse. Everybody did.

And touch none unclean thing, she said. That’s the half that’s the going out.

And what is my unclean thing, Goodwife? I’ve no cows. I’ve no glass in my window.

You counted your shillings twice on the landing, she said. I heard about it from three people. And you count the places you’ve come out of on your fingers, the same way, twice, to anybody that will sit still for it. The bishop and your brother and Boston and the island and us. You carry it out of every house you go out of, like they carry their kettles to Duxbury. It came over in the ship with you. It’s the one thing you never left.

He got up off the bench so hard it went over, and went out into the garden.

III. The Second Noise

Half an hour after

He sat on her chopping block among the bean poles with his back to the house. He was going to go home. He would go home and not come in again, and that would be one more place, and he caught himself putting it on the hand with the others.

The second noise came then, out of the north the same way, but lower, and the ground went under him once like a horse shifting its feet, not so strong as the first. Up and down the street he heard the women cry out and run for their posts again. He did not take hold of anything. He sat on the block with his hands on his knees and let it come through him and go on south toward Manomet and the Cape, and he saw the thing.

He could never afterward say it better than that. He had been looking out from it all his life, the way a man looks out from a window and never sees the glass, and now for the space of the shaking it was outside him and he was looking at it. It was small. It was the size of a sparrow. It had come out of the bishop’s church with him and out of his brother’s door and off the ship at Boston and down the path to the neck, and it was fed, and loud, and it was not Rowland Fenn. It was only a thing he had been carrying. He had five places on his hand, and it had been with him at every one of them, and he had never once gone out of anything.

The shaking stopped. A hen came down off the woodpile. He found that he was laughing, and that his face was wet, and that it did not matter which.

•••

When he came back in, the widow was sweeping up the clay that had come down the chimney. He did not say anything about it, and she did not ask. He went out again to Thomas Ware’s old shop at the back, where the shaving horse still stood with the cobwebs on it, and found a riven lath and a hammer and five nails in a dish, and he came in and nailed the lath along the front of the shelf over the hearth a thumb’s height above the board. She handed him the platters one at a time and he stood them behind it. The dented one he put at the end where she could reach it.

The shaking may come again, he said. They say there’ll be more.

It may, she said.

The small shakings did come, for near three weeks, a little one most days, and nobody in the town could get used to them. He walked home that evening over the Jones River with no kids on the cord and the purse in his coat, and did not count it.

•••

The summers after that year were cold and wet, for several years together, and the Indian corn often did not ripen. In 1640 the price fell all at once, the way the old planters had feared, so that a cow that was worth twenty pound one month would fetch five the next, and a milch goat that had gone at three pound would not bring ten shillings. The removals went on anyway. Rowland Fenn kept his goats on the neck. He never did come to meeting, and nobody in Plymouth ever called him anything but the goat man. But he came into town every week with milk for whoever was sick, and when the Widow Ware lay dying in the winter of 1645, it was he that sat with her the last three nights and spooned the broth.

Her grandson had the house after. The lath was still on the shelf. When the ground moved again, in the winter of 1663, he heard the platters talk to each other in the dark, and lean, and stay where they were.

Background

The verse

Because a man is rid of evil, therefore he is called Brahmana; because he walks quietly, therefore he is called Samana; because he has sent away his own impurities, therefore he is called Pravragita (Pabbagita, a pilgrim).Dhammapada 388, tr. F. Max Müller (1881)

In the commentary, a brahmin who had gone forth as an ascetic under some other teacher heard that the Buddha called his own monks pabbajita, “those who have gone forth,” and reasoned that the name ought to fit him too, since he had also left home. He went to the Buddha and said so. The Buddha answered that nobody is called gone forth for leaving a house; a person is called gone forth because the stains have gone forth out of him, and spoke the verse, which explains each holy name by a pun on what has been put away. The brahmin reached the first stage of awakening as he listened. Read the full commentary story, or about pabbajjā, the going forth.

Plymouth, 1638

The Separatists of Plymouth Colony had come out of the Church of England, out of Nottinghamshire to Amsterdam in 1608, then to Leiden, then over the sea in 1620. In the 1630s the great migration into Massachusetts Bay drove up the price of livestock, and William Bradford recorded kine selling at £20 to £28, a milch goat at £3 or £4, and female kids at 30 to 40 shillings. Planters who had stock went out to their outlying grants for meadow, the Duxbury people were dismissed to form their own church, and Bradford feared the scattering would “be the ruin of New England.” In 1640 cattle prices collapsed: a cow that had been worth £20 fell to £5. Bradford’s own account is in his history of Plimoth Plantation. Rowland Fenn’s Boston is the town of the Antinomian Controversy, after which Anne Hutchinson’s supporters were disarmed and many went to Aquidneck Island in 1638.

Bradford also describes the earthquake of about the first of June, 1638 (June 11 by the modern calendar): heard before it was felt, like distant thunder out of the north, shaking platters and dishes off the shelves while “the cheefe of this towne were mett together at one house” with friends who were removing, so that women outdoors had to catch hold of posts and pales, with a second, weaker shock about half an hour later. He noted that the summers for several years afterward were cold. The sermon the widow quotes was given at Plymouth in December 1621 by Robert Cushman, a layman and the colony’s agent, during his month-long visit on the Fortune; it was printed in London in 1622 without his name. The text here follows the Boston reprint of 1846, The Sin and Danger of Self-Love.
