Findings (run of 2026-11-11)

  1. The seed is as big a pitch lever as the words. Re-rolling the seed with the same instruction moves median pitch by about 2.7 semitones (SD). With no instruction, Ryan's three takes came out at 111, 119 and 171 Hz. Any single take says little; compare words over several seeds, or fix the seed.
  2. Instructions mostly push pitch up, rarely down. The no-instruction voice sits near the bottom of each speaker's range. Even neutral or nonsense words in the frame “Speak in a ___ voice.” raise pitch about +2.6 semitones, while “Read this sentence aloud.” does not. Judge a word against that dashed template line, not against zero.
  3. Arousal moves pitch as much as pitch words do. “frightened”, “shouting”, “angry” and “excited” land with “squeaky”, “falsetto” and “high-pitched” (+6 to +8 st). “booming” and “gravelly” also go up, as more energetic voices, despite meaning low or rough.
  4. Lowering is weak and speaker-dependent. Only “deep”, “calm”, “monotone” and “low-pitched” go below the template line, and only Ryan gets clearly below his default (“deep” −4.3 st). No instruction took Vivian meaningfully below her default. “bass”, “baritone”, “elderly” and “giant's” do nothing useful.
  5. Dose words don't grade. “slightly higher” ≈ “higher” (about +6 st); “much higher” is the top (+9). Downward, “slightly / much / extremely lower” are all within noise of each other.
  6. Low-arousal words narrow the intonation, not just the pitch. calm, sad, sleepy, monotone and deep shrink the pitch range to roughly 7–9 st from 10–14 st; sad and sleepy also slow Vivian to about 135 wpm.
  7. No linear pitch direction in the instruction embeddings. A direction built from high-pitch minus low-pitch word embeddings only loosely predicts measured shifts (r ≈ 0.6 on pitch words, 0.35 overall), is nearly orthogonal to arousal, age, gender and size directions (|cos| ≤ 0.16, vs 0.12 for random word pairs), and adding it to a neutral instruction (up to a vector longer than a token embedding) barely moves pitch (+0.2 st per unit, not significant over six seeds). The arousal-to-pitch coupling is built inside the transformer, from words in context, not carried by the raw word vectors.

For the narration voice: to keep a low, even narrator, use low-arousal style words (“calm”, “unhurried”, “deep”), avoid the “Speak in a ___ voice” frame, and audition several seeds, then pin the seed you like. To push lower than the default, prefer a different speaker or a cloned reference over instruction words.

Next step if you want more: read the talker's hidden states at the instruction positions (layer by layer) and fit a probe for measured F0 there; that is where the arousal–pitch link should show up.
